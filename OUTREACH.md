# Research Outreach Brief

## Short pitch

**Agent Continuity Recognition** is an open protocol for testing whether a long-term AI agent remains recognizably continuous after model swaps, compression, migration, or handoff.

The design separates three things that are often conflated:

1. whether state and lineage persist;
2. whether behavior remains stable;
3. whether people who know the agent well can recognize the continuation under blinded conditions.

A key control is a **factual-memory-matched decoy**: a fresh agent receives the same biography and salient memories, so recognition cannot be passed merely by recalling the right past.

## Why a lab might care

The protocol can be run with existing agent infrastructure and does not require adopting a theory of consciousness or personhood.

It is useful for groups studying:

- persistent agents;
- model migration or backend substitution;
- long-term memory systems;
- persona drift;
- human calibration to behavioral changes;
- longitudinal human-AI relationships;
- evaluation of agent identity or lineage.

## What we are asking

We are looking for research groups willing to:

- run a pilot or full replication;
- critique the design publicly;
- preregister modifications;
- report null results;
- preserve protocol provenance and cite the archived version.

## One-paragraph email version

> We have published an open protocol for testing AI-agent continuity across model swaps, compression, migration, and handoff. The design separates mechanical lineage, behavioral stability, and blinded human recognition, and includes factual-memory-matched decoys so a candidate cannot succeed merely by knowing the same biography. We are looking for groups with persistent-agent infrastructure or longitudinal user populations who may want to pilot, critique, or independently replicate the design. The protocol is open, versioned, and intended for DOI archiving and preregistration.
